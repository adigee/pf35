Every EU member state is set to ship an EUDI wallet. But what will people use it for? And where will it be most valuable? That's an open question and uncovering it is a meaningful design challenge.

For example, is proving your identity in the middle of a payment a valuable use-case? And if it is, how would it work in a way that protects privacy, enhances convenience (for buyer and seller) and drives adoption of a particular wallet?

By introducing digital identity in its wallet, Wero has the opportunity to introduce the benefits of eIDAS 2.0 to a continent of consumers with seamless UX. I've worked on problems like this. New patterns, made to feel obvious.

Designing an EUDI wallet

Starting with the ecosystem

As the centerpiece in a complex ecosystem, an EUDI wallet sits in a network of identity providers, service providers, regulatory frameworks. To start, I would move beyond the screens to how data flows, how trust is established and map the system overall.

I've worked across both B2B and B2C contexts, including a stint in regulated environments such as insurance sector. Adding my experience in service design, this allows me to approach this with a holistic lens, not just as a product, but as a system of interactions across multiple nodes.

Customer acquisition

Everyday transactions leading to everyday adoption

The current conversation talks about opening a bank account in a new country or buying wine in an online retailer, use-cases that are real but rare. Using qual discovery to uncover real world use-cases will be key to drive more adoption against native wallets (Apple, Google, Samsung).

The biggest challenges to adoption are behavioral not technical. Consent fatigue, lack of mental models, trust ambiguity are few that come to mind.

Design principles

Trust is accumulated

The thing that struck me about designing an EUDI wallet is that it needs to be valuable and accessible to basically everyone. With the customer base being a diverse continent of consumers, uncovering the contexts and abilities would be fundamental.

Visual accessibility will be baked in from the design system but beyond this, designing a trust surface would require looking at the whole product experience.
