Why the current marker goes dark at the tail

components/text_highlight.js draws one hand-drawn swipe (viewBox 0 0 137 25) and stretches it to the phrase's exact box with preserveAspectRatio="none" — width scales, height doesn't. A darker masked "cap" near x 127–134 (~5% of the shape) is meant to read as the pen lifting off the page. Because that cap is baked into the same stretchy path as the body, its pixel width scales with however long the phrase is — on a short phrase it's a small, believable tip; on a long one it stretches into a wide, smeared dark patch at the trailing (right, in LTR) edge. That's the "dark edge towards the back." A below reproduces this exactly; B–E are different fixes.

Update — B2 shipped, then a new artifact turned up. B2 mirrors the shape's own left-edge taper onto the right so both ends match. But that taper is a proportional cut — roughly 11% of the 137-unit viewBox — carved diagonally across the full height, from the top edge down to the bottom edge in close to a straight line. Because preserveAspectRatio="none" scales width independently of height, that diagonal's on-screen length grows with phrase length while its steepness flattens out — on real case-study copy it stretches into a long, shallow, dead-straight bevel at each end instead of a short, steep, wobbly one. Combined with the hard rectangular mask cut that crops it, the outline reads as flat facets meeting at sharp corners — a polygon, not a rounded swipe. That's the "pentagon." What happens if the phrase wraps to a second line?

Short answer: today it can't break mid-phrase — components/text_highlight.js sets white-space: nowrap on the highlight span, so the browser treats the whole marked phrase as one atomic unit. On a narrow column it either jumps down to its own line as a whole, or — if even a full line isn't wide enough — overflows past the edge. If that guard were removed so the phrase could wrap internally, the single stretchy SVG background doesn't know about the line break: it just stretches across the full two-line bounding box, ignoring the actual ragged shape of the wrapped text underneath.
