Introduction

A global leader creating a 2 sided marketplace for luggage storage — Bounce enables travelers to store their bags in a local shop for a simple 5€ a day. With locations in over 100+ countries, Bounce had built a supply network with density in touristic city centres and airbnb dense neighbourhoods.

The inevitable next stage in growing the supply network was to enable customers to store luggage at airports, train stations, bus stations but the marketplace model would not work here.

In the summer 2024, I joined a PM and an engineer on the New Verticals team to build out the lockers product experience (later known as lockers PUDO).

My role

My team was responsible for demonstrating how lockers would enable Bounce to enter transportation hubs and acquire the demand at the source. While most designers in Bounce either worked on demand or supply side of the marketplace, I was responsible for the end to end customer journey for lockers customers.

I worked on acquisition, post-booking and support product experiences for lockers. I was also driving a continuous discovery practice that enabled the team to stay on top of the feedback and iterate quickly.

Problem definition

Lockers is the natural customer expectation for luggage storage. However we still needed to help customers select the best location, find the locker, open it all through the Bounce app. By creating this product experience, we needed to prove that Bounce could provide a valuable service at transportation hubs.

Approach

By launching with existing parcel locker networks, we were able to build and test the lockers product experience and prepare the Bounce app for a self service experience. It also enabled us to test the locker hypothesis and evaluate the demand we were acquiring near train stations.

Later in Q1 of 2025, we launched our first proprietary location that enabled us to further our thesis in launching and managing lockers as a business.

Continuous discovery

Since lockers was new to us and everyone at Bounce, all we had was assumptions and opinions. We saw all the cancellations coming in but we didn't know why. Where were the gaps in the customer journey? What were the main pain points?

I ran customer interviews every week to learn about what customers were doing out in the wild. Sharing these qualitative snippets helped me sharpen our product intuition and map out the customer's mental model.

Design deepdives

Parcel lockers have a 90 second timeout once the locker door is open. Customers would often open the locker, repack their bags and only store it, resulting in a timeout. Many iterations later, introducing a timer helped our customers feel the time constraint.

Outcomes

Over the course of Q4 and Q1, we steadily worked on reducing the cancellation rate. By the end of Q1, we had launched 6 networks in Japan, Germany, Austria, France, UK and Brazil.
